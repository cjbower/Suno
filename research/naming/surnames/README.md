# Surnames

**Purpose**

This folder contains surname research, focusing on the most common surnames in the United States and their origins.

**Contents**

- top-50-us-surnames.md – Most common surnames ranked by frequency

**Methodology**

Data derived from U.S. Census Bureau records and surname frequency databases.

Each entry includes:
- Rank (1-50)
- Surname
- Common origin (when determinable)

**How to Use**

1. Review most common surnames to understand baseline "familiarity"
2. Use for collision analysis (extremely common surnames may increase collision risk when combined with common first names)
3. Identify generational/cultural branding (e.g., "Smith" vs. "Cruz" vs. "Wang")
4. Support surname selection for artist/band naming

**Collision Analysis**

Extremely common name combinations (e.g., "Michael Smith") carry higher collision risk:
- More difficult to search and distinguish online
- More likely to have industry matches
- Harder to build unique brand recognition

Mid-frequency surnames combined with common first names provide:
- Good searchability
- Lower collision risk
- Memorable combinations
- Professional branding potential

**Limitations**

- Reflects primarily U.S. patterns
- Census data has time lag
- Does not capture adopted names or preferred names
- Ethnic/cultural surnames may be underrepresented relative to demographics
- Surname frequency changes over time

**Research Gaps**

- International surname databases not included
- Generational surname adoption patterns not covered
- Regional variation not fully captured
- Recent immigrant name patterns under-represented
