# Top 50 US Surnames

**Purpose**

This dataset documents the top 50 most common surnames in the United States based on U.S. Census data.

**Source**

U.S. Census Bureau – Surnames in the 2010 U.S. Census (most recent comprehensive data)

**Methodology**

Ranked by frequency of occurrence across the entire U.S. population. Each entry includes rank, surname, and common origin when available from census documentation.

**Collision Risk Analysis by Category**

**EXTREME COLLISION (Ranks 1-5)**
- Smith, Johnson, Williams, Brown, Jones
- These five surnames account for significant percentage of U.S. population
- Very difficult to establish unique identity with common first names
- Recommendation: Avoid if possible; requires significant differentiation

**HIGH COLLISION (Ranks 6-20)**
- Garcia, Miller, Davis, Rodriguez, Martinez, Hernandez, Lopez, Gonzalez, Wilson, Anderson, Thomas, Taylor, Moore, Jackson, Martin
- Common surnames that increase collision risk
- Searchability compromised with common first names
- Recommendation: Use with distinctive first names or branding

**MEDIUM COLLISION (Ranks 21-40)**
- Lee, Perez, Thompson, White, Harris, Sanchez, Clark, Ramirez, Lewis, Robinson, Walker, Young, Hall, Flores, Green, Nelson, Carter, Roberts, Phillips, Campbell
- Moderate collision risk
- Adequate searchability with distinctive first names
- Recommendation: Usable with strategic first-name selection

**LOW-TO-MEDIUM COLLISION (Ranks 41-50)**
- Parker, Evans, Edwards, Collins, Reyes, Stewart, Morris, Morales, Vega, Alvarado
- Lower collision risk
- Good searchability potential
- Recommendation: Good choices for unique artist identities

**Generational/Cultural Distribution**

**Anglo-Saxon Surnames (English/Northern European origin)**
- Ranks 1-5: Smith, Johnson, Williams, Brown, Jones dominate
- Traditional surnames with 400+ years of U.S. history
- Highest collision risk

**Spanish/Hispanic Surnames (Ranks 6-13 and 22-26)**
- Garcia, Rodriguez, Martinez, Hernandez, Lopez, Gonzalez (top Hispanic surnames)
- Growing demographic representation
- Good cultural branding for Latin/Latin-influenced music

**Occupational Surnames (Ranks 7, 17, 31, 34, 38, 42, 47)**
- Miller, Taylor, Walker, Hall, Carter, Parker, Stewart
- Historical origin (occupational designation)
- Distinctive branding potential

**Descriptive Surnames (Ranks 4, 24, 32, 36)**
- Brown, White, Young, Green
- Relatively common but offer some distinctiveness

**Limitations**

- 2010 Census data (most recent comprehensive data)
- Reflects primarily U.S. population
- Does not capture recent immigration name patterns
- Does not reflect hyphenated or compound surnames
- May not reflect rapid demographic changes 2010-2024

**Research Gaps**

- International surname patterns not covered
- Hyphenated surnames (increasingly common) not included
- Recent immigrant surnames not fully represented
- Regional surname variations not documented
- Generational surname adoption patterns not covered
