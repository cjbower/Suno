**Purpose**

This dataset documents the top 20 most popular male first names born during the 2010s in the United States.

**Source**

U.S. Social Security Administration – National Popular Baby Names Database (births 2010-2019)

**Methodology**

Ranked by frequency of occurrence in Social Security Administration birth records.

**Generational Branding Context**

SIGNIFICANT SHIFT: The 2010s shows dramatic departure from previous decades. Traditional names (Michael, Jason, Robert) drop from top positions. Modern, literary, and contemporary names rise. These names represent Gen-Z (born 2010-2024).

**Key Observations**

- Liam, Noah, Oliver are NEW top names (not in previous decades)
- Michael drops from #1 (1970s-2000s) to #12 (2010s)
- Jason completely absent from top 20
- Robert completely absent from top 20
- Contemporary/literary names (Elijah, Oliver, Liam) now dominate
- Traditional names (William, James) remain but as mid-range options

**Collision Risk**

DIFFERENT PATTERN: Liam, Noah, Oliver carry high collision risk in Gen-Z context but LOW collision risk when combined with older surname databases. This creates interesting branding opportunity: familiar to Gen-Z but searchable across generations.
