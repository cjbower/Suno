# Name Trends Analysis

**Status:** Evidence-limited review. The previous edition's demographic, generational, cultural, and collision conclusions are withdrawn because its underlying calculations and source extracts were not reproducible.

## Scope and Evidence

The local first-name files contain static top-20 lists for the 1970s through 2020s. They do not retain the underlying annual counts, a reproducible extraction, or enough source metadata to validate decade aggregation. The 2020s files specify 2020-2024 only and are partial. The [official data-source review](official-name-data-source-review.md) records current access limits and the appropriate official sources.

The lists may be retained as historical working material, but this report does not certify their ranks or treat them as current source data. The prior statements that a decade marks a definitive generational break, that specific names are familiar to age groups, or that a surname implies cultural fit or collision advantage are not supported by the retained evidence and must not guide roster decisions.

## What Name Rankings Can and Cannot Show

With a documented, comparable source and method, aggregate name counts can describe the frequency of names in a defined geography, period, and source-reported category. They can support carefully bounded historical comparison.

They cannot, by themselves, establish:

- The age, gender identity, ethnicity, language, culture, personality, or audience of a person or fictional artist.
- Whether a name feels contemporary, authentic, familiar, or suitable to listeners.
- Whether an artist name, band name, trademark, handle, domain, or platform identity is available.
- A causal account of why a naming pattern changed, or a forecast of future popularity.

Source category labels must remain source-specific. Birth-certificate sex categories and a dashboard's `boy` / `girl` labels are not interchangeable, and neither describes an individual person's gender identity.

## Method Needed for Valid Trend Claims

Before making a trend claim, retain or identify:

1. The authoritative source and its exact retrieval date.
2. The source's geographic basis, birth years, category definitions, inclusion rules, and suppression or top-N limits.
3. Counts by birth year, rather than only ordinal ranks.
4. A stated aggregation method. For a multi-year period, sum comparable annual counts; do not average ordinal ranks.
5. Data transformations, tie handling, validation against source-published results, and known omissions.
6. A clear distinction between descriptive frequency and any separate audience or branding hypothesis.

See the [Official Baby-Name Data Source Review](official-name-data-source-review.md) for evaluated SSA, California CDPH, Washington DOH, and CDC/NCHS sources.

## Implications for Studio Naming

- Do not change or assign any current artist, band, or project name based on this analysis. Existing names remain as recorded.
- Use aggregate popularity only as limited historical context or an ideation aid, not as a score for identity, authenticity, audience fit, or availability.
- Do not infer a person's culture, language, ethnicity, or musical genre from a first or last name.
- Treat cultural, linguistic, and pronunciation questions as matters for context-specific human review, not demographic classification.
- Assess collision risk through dated, direct searches of relevant music services, identifiers, domains, social accounts, and trademark sources. Record the scope and limits of each search.
- Audience familiarity claims require separately designed, consent-governed research; a birth-name dataset is not a listener study.

## Research Boundary

No underlying name data was refreshed or downloaded for this review. SSA access returned HTTP 403 and the California data portal returned HTTP 502; the California data dictionary was accessible and its definitions are summarized in the source review. No individual birth records are used or requested.
