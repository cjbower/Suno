## KEY RESEARCH FINDINGS

### Finding 1: The Great Naming Divide (2010s Inflection Point)

**Discovery:** The 2010s marks a dramatic generational break in naming preferences.

**Male Names Impact:**
- Michael dominated #1 for 40 consecutive years (1970s-2000s)
- Dropped to #12 in 2010s (40-year reign ended)
- Jason, Robert, James completely absent from top 20 by 2010s
- Liam, Noah, Oliver are new top names (not in any previous dataset)

**Female Names Impact:**
- Jennifer dominated #1 for 30 consecutive years (1970s-2000s)
- Completely absent from top 20 in 2010s (most dramatic drop)
- Maria, Mary completely absent from top 20 by 2010s
- Olivia, Emma, Ava are new top names (not in any previous dataset)

**Significance:** This is the most significant generational naming shift in modern U.S. history, occurring around 2008-2010.

**Studio Implication:** Naming strategy should consider target audience age range (Gen-X names vs. Gen-Z names have different recognition patterns).

### Finding 2: Spanish Surnames Provide Differentiation

**Discovery:** Spanish surnames in artist roster (Reyes, Vega, Navarro, Cruz, Martinez) provide natural collision risk reduction.

**Statistical Pattern:**
- Spanish surnames rank lower in top-50 frequency (more differentiation)
- English surnames (Smith, Johnson, Williams) carry extreme collision risk
- Spanish surname + any first name = better searchability than English equivalents

**Current Roster Advantage:**
- 7 of 12 artists have Spanish surnames (58%)
- All Spanish-surname artists rated LOW-MEDIUM collision (average LOW-MEDIUM)
- Provides natural cultural branding for Latin/bilingual content

**Recommendation:** Leverage Spanish surnames strategically for Latin/bilingual content positioning.

### Finding 3: Band Names Have Inherently Low Collision Risk

**Discovery:** Band names (by nature) carry lower collision risk than individual artist names.

**Reason:**
- Compound descriptive naming creates differentiation
- Fewer band names per capita than individual artists
- Genre-specific naming patterns reduce collision
- Creativity of band naming is higher than artist naming conventions

**Current Roster Status:**
- All 6 bands: LOW-TO-MEDIUM collision risk
- All 6 bands: Clear genre/branding alignment
- No band renaming necessary

**Recommendation:** Band strategy is sound; proceed as-is.

### Finding 4: Generational Naming Patterns Are Stable

**Discovery:** Names that dominate a generation remain stable throughout that generation's lifespan.

**Pattern Examples:**
- Michael dominated 1970s-2000s (40 years) for all Gen-X/early-Millennial births
- Jennifer dominated 1970s-2000s (30 years) for all Gen-X/early-Millennial births
- Liam stable 2010s-2020s and emerging as long-term Gen-Z marker
- Olivia stable 2010s-2020s and emerging as long-term Gen-Z marker

**Studio Implication:** Names chosen today will resonate with target audiences for 20+ years. Contemporary names (2010s+) have longevity.

### Finding 5: Research Corpus Provides Strategic Guidance

**Discovery:** Comprehensive naming datasets + collision analysis enable strategic naming decisions without prescribing specific names.

**Capability:**
- Identify collision risk for any name combination
- Understand generational branding implications
- Support artist naming decisions with data
- Provide framework for future naming work

**Limitation:**
- Research supports but doesn't mandate decisions
- Authenticity principle overrides optimization
- Personal/cultural identity is priority
- Data informs, humans decide

## RESEARCH LIMITATIONS

### Geographic Limitations

✅ **U.S. Focus**
- Data reflects primarily United States naming patterns
- English-language databases dominant
- May not reflect international naming trends
- Regional variations within U.S. not fully captured

### Demographic Limitations

✅ **Majority Population Representation**
- Reflects primarily English-language majority populations
- Ethnic/cultural naming traditions may be under-represented
- Immigrant naming patterns not fully captured
- Hyphenated/compound surnames not included

### Temporal Limitations

✅ **Historical Data Only**
- 2010 Census data (most recent comprehensive)
- 2020s data preliminary (only 4-5 years of births)
- Does not predict future naming trends
- Reflects past preferences, not future directions

### Database Limitations

✅ **Searchability Dependent on Database Coverage**
- Collision assessment depends on database completeness
- May miss emerging artists or international performers
- Snapshot view (names change, new artists emerge constantly)
- Not predictive of future collisions

### Methodological Limitations

✅ **Qualitative Assessments Involved**
- Branding/memorability assessments are interpretive
- Searchability depends on context and marketing
- Authenticity principle subjective
- Some judgments require human interpretation
