# Name Selection Strategy

**Purpose:** Provide a human-led framework for evaluating future artist, band, and project names.
**Authority:** Research guidance only. It does not approve, assign, or change any name.

## Principles

1. **Respect identity and authorship.** A personal or artist-selected name is not an optimization variable. Do not pressure a person to change a name for search ranking, demographic fit, or branding.
2. **Serve the creative purpose.** A name should be considered in context of the work, source, language, and intended artistic identity. It must not be used to imply an unsupported biography or real-person identity.
3. **Check clarity with context.** Pronunciation, spelling, recall, and visual use are review questions, not universal traits. Ask the relevant people and audiences rather than assuming.
4. **Verify distinctiveness directly.** Name-frequency data is not collision evidence. Search current music services, artist identifiers, domains, social platforms, and relevant trademark records; document date, query, result, and limitations.
5. **Avoid cultural inference.** Do not infer ethnicity, language, religion, genre, audience, or authenticity from a first or last name. Cultural positioning requires a source-based rationale and appropriate human review.
6. **Keep the decision reversible until approved.** Candidate names remain proposals. Record the decision, rationale, unresolved risks, and approver before any canonical roster or metadata change.

## Appropriate Use of Name-Frequency Data

Official aggregate baby-name datasets can provide limited historical context about names recorded in a defined place, period, and source category. They may help generate candidates, but they do not prove familiarity, contemporary appeal, collision risk, identity, audience preference, or cultural fit.

Use the [Official Baby-Name Data Source Review](official-name-data-source-review.md) and [Name Trends Analysis](name-trends-analysis.md) before citing rankings. Preserve the source's own category labels and coverage. Do not infer individual characteristics from aggregate data.

## Review Workflow

1. Record the proposed name and its source or author, if applicable.
2. Confirm the name does not assert an unapproved real-person identity, affiliation, endorsement, or performance history.
3. Review language, pronunciation, cultural context, accessibility, and possible unintended meanings with appropriate human input.
4. Conduct and document current collision and availability checks. Popularity rankings are not a substitute.
5. Review rights, trademark, contract, and platform-specific questions with the appropriate human or professional reviewer.
6. Obtain explicit human approval before changing a canonical name, folder, registry, release, or public-facing profile.
7. Preserve the prior record and decision history when an approved change is made.

## Current Roster Boundary

This strategy makes no changes to the current artist, band, or producer-project names and does not establish biographies, ages, voices, genres, audience segments, or cultural assignments. Existing collision assessments are time-sensitive and should be treated as unverified until repeated using documented current searches.
