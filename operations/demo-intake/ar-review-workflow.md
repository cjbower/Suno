# A&R Review Workflow

## Role

A&R (Artists and Repertoire) evaluates repertoire opportunities and recommends whether and how a demo should be developed. It does not engineer audio, administer focus groups, own catalog metadata, or operate releases.

## Review

Use the [demo review template](demo-review-template.md) and [evaluation standard](evaluation-standard.md). Grade each criterion A, B, C, D, F, or N/R and include written evidence. C is a valid midpoint; the overall creative grade is an editorial judgment, not an arithmetic average.

Separate:

- Creative strength and identifiable assets
- Source fidelity and rights conditions
- Technical accessibility and known defects
- Fit with an existing entity, a provisional identity candidate, or unassigned repertoire
- Development cost, risks, and evidence needed

One strong element may justify preserving a demo even when its overall disposition is hold, decline, or archive.

## Decisions

A&R may recommend any disposition in the [disposition standard](disposition-standard.md). An assignment, new identity evaluation, collaboration, licensing, or handoff recommendation is not itself authorization to create a record, contact a party, or begin production.

## Development Boundary

Internal Studio Development begins only after an authorized human approves a formal Development Brief. The Brief names a project owner, preserves source lineage, defines permitted and prohibited changes, identifies rights conditions, and specifies success evidence.

## Final Authority

Human editorial review decides whether to revise, hold, assign, promote, hand off, or archive. Audience-research findings may inform but never determine the decision.
