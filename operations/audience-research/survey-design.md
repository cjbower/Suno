# Survey Design

## Instrument Governance

Freeze and identify each survey version before collecting responses. Preserve descriptive wording, scale endpoints, order, and any deliberate changes. The survey is not launched in this phase.

## Sections

### A. Listener Context

- Anonymous participant ID
- Cohort
- Optional age band
- Genre familiarity
- Source-material familiarity
- Playback method
- Listening environment

Do not request names, email addresses, birth dates, or other direct identifiers.

### B. Immediate Response

- First-impression grade
- Continue-listening response
- Most memorable moment
- One-word reaction

### C. Letter-Grade Evaluation

Use A, B, C, D, F, or N/R for:

- Emotional impact
- Memorability
- Voice
- Production
- Arrangement
- Lyrics
- Authenticity
- Replay interest

See the [evaluation standard](evaluation-standard.md).

### D. Semantic Differential

Display descriptive endpoints for each bipolar dimension:

- Forgettable – Memorable
- Generic – Distinctive
- Artificial – Human
- Dated – Contemporary
- Emotionally Flat – Emotionally Affecting
- Weak – Powerful
- Confusing – Clear
- Unlikely to Replay – Likely to Replay

Preserve the middle position unless a future study deliberately approves forced choice. Any internal numerical encoding must not replace the displayed language.

### E. Behavioral Intent

- Would listen again
- Would add to a playlist
- Would share
- Would listen to the artist again
- Would listen to a companion podcast
- Preferred listening context

### F. Open Response

- Strongest element
- Weakest element
- What should not change
- What should change
- Anything misunderstood

## Method Notes

Ask context questions without implying a preferred answer. Separate independent responses from optional group discussion. Record instrument version and limitations so later changes do not silently invalidate comparison.
