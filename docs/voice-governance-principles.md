# Voice Governance Principles

1. **Voice and artist are separate concepts.** Manage their identities and records independently.
2. **Artists may outlive voice implementations.** A change in voice technology does not by itself change the artist.
3. **Voice changes require documentation.** Record the reason, review, and resulting version or replacement.
4. **Reproducibility is preferred over novelty.** Preserve enough version and implementation context to understand prior work.
5. **Catalog continuity is more important than experimentation.** New voice experiments must not silently alter established catalog references.
6. **Human review remains authoritative.** Automated similarity or drift checks inform decisions but do not approve them.
7. **Real-person voice use requires explicit, scoped authorization.** Public availability, technical access, or a platform setting is not consent; separate rights review is required. See [Real-Person Voice Consent and Rights Governance](real-voice-consent-and-rights-governance.md).
