# Manifesto Delta Report

Purpose: Record how the Version 2 working manifesto relates to the original Version 0.9 document and identify the structural and architectural changes made during Phase 1.

## Preserved Concepts

- The project is an enduring creative ecosystem, not a sequence of isolated AI-generated songs.
- Recognizable artists, consistent voices, production DNA, thematic continuity, and evolving catalogs are central.
- Source material and narrative meaning should lead creative decisions; historical and scriptural material should not feel like a lecture by default.
- Contemporary, playlist-compatible production is encouraged, while Broadway, musical theater, cinematic-trailer excess, robotic vocals, excessive processing, and spectacle for their own sake are discouraged.
- Clean recordings, conversational delivery, minimal vocal processing, emotional connection, and instrumental breathing room remain useful production preferences.
- Psalms/spiritual work and historical/civic work are distinct initiatives that may share infrastructure.
- Music and podcasts can complement each other through context, reflection, and storytelling.
- Songs may accumulate before related work is curated into albums or playlists.
- The underlying motivation is cultural, educational, creative, and spiritual stewardship, with income recognized as legitimate support for the work.

## Expanded Concepts

- The artist-led model is expressed as a label-to-initiative-to-artist-to-release/catalog relationship, with shared infrastructure and distinct editorial purposes.
- The proposed roster names are recorded as placeholders, without invented biographies, voices, genres, audiences, or initiative assignments.
- Genre is defined as an open-ended portfolio, with the supplied initial genre families retained and room for subgenres, hybrids, and language/context attributes.
- Emotional intent is made into a reviewable editorial sequence from source identification through interpretation, artist assignment, production, and review.
- Artist and voice continuity is framed as versioned identity stewardship, with stable traits distinguished from documented evolution.
- Commercial-quality aspirations are translated into review dimensions while explicitly avoiding any promise of chart outcomes.
- Source handling is expanded to cover provenance, editions, translations, adaptation notes, attribution, and rights review.
- Catalog-first development now includes traceable relationships, stable identifiers as a future requirement, and links among source, artist, release, and companion media.
- Artist lifecycle stewardship includes preservation across development, activity, pause, and retirement while leaving statuses and approval criteria open.
- Future automation is bounded to administrative support with visible uncertainty, traceability, and human editorial approval.
- Repository governance makes historical preservation, additive change, explicit placeholders, and non-destructive stewardship explicit.

## New Concepts

- Editorial Vision, Label Structure, Emotional Objective Framework, Billboard-Level Quality Standards, Source Material Strategy, Historical Initiative, Catalog Development, Artist Lifecycle Management, Future Automation Opportunities, Repository Governance, and Long-Term Mission are explicit standalone sections.
- Latin, Regional Mexican, bilingual, and multilingual opportunities are called out with a need for cultural and language review, without assigning them to a particular artist.
- Rights and permission review is identified as necessary for source texts, translations, editions, contributors, voice/identity use, music, and podcast material; the document does not make legal determinations.
- Human-review questions are gathered in one place to prevent open matters from being mistaken for settled policy.
- The later-phase directory plan is recorded as a conceptual repository architecture, distinct from files and folders actually created during Phase 1.

## Structural Changes

The original manifesto is preserved unchanged as historical context. Version 2 reorganizes its narrative notes into a working design with sixteen explicitly requested sections, followed by open decisions for human review. Repeated ideas from the original are consolidated under editorial, roster, production, source, initiative, podcast, catalog, lifecycle, and governance topics. The original Ticonderoga example and production discoveries remain represented as historical context and guidance rather than being elevated into universal requirements. No repository structure or artist content was generated as part of this Phase 1 report.

## Architectural Decisions

- `Suno Creative Studio` is treated as the umbrella label and stewardship layer; this is a creative/repository model, not a legal-entity decision.
- Psalms and Historical are separate initial editorial initiatives with shared infrastructure permitted.
- The artist roster is persistent and identity-led. The twelve supplied names are proposed placeholders, not approved profiles.
- Source material informs emotional objective; the objective informs artist assignment; artist identity may influence genre and production.
- Genre families are not privileged or excluded, and the supplied list is an initial taxonomy rather than a limit.
- Catalog records should link sources, artists, initiatives, tracks, releases, versions, contributors, rights evidence, and companion media where applicable.
- Individual songs may precede album formation; collections should emerge through intentional curation.
- The repository is the intended authoritative record. The proposed Phase 2 directory plan is documented but not yet implemented.
- Human editorial review remains authoritative; automation is a future opportunity, not an approved integration or autonomous creative decision-maker.

## Outstanding Questions

- Which artist names, identities, initiative assignments, and roster size are approved?
- What legal structure, label/imprint treatment, ownership model, and contributor agreements will apply?
- Which source editions and translations may be used, and what rights/attribution review and approval process is required?
- What language, dialect, and cultural review is required for Latin, bilingual, and other multilingual releases?
- Who owns editorial, artist identity, production, rights, and release approvals?
- What quality references, technical delivery thresholds, review gates, and exceptions should be adopted?
- What release cadence, distribution approach, audience research, and podcast formats are appropriate?
- What lifecycle statuses, activation/retirement criteria, and reactivation policy should be used?
- What identifiers, metadata schemas, repository roles, backup/recovery practices, and change-control process will be adopted?
- Which automation, if any, should be evaluated after the repository workflow is established?
