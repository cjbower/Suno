## Executive Summary

This phase completes the strategic documentation for a dynamic, event-driven media end state that complements rather than replaces permanent catalog music. It records the source-to-lyrics model, preference dimensions, sports storytelling examples, lifecycle, direct audience and portability thesis, early-implementation hypothesis, provider/company boundary, latency and quality measures, and rights, safety, and editorial gates.

The approved decision is [DEC-2B6-04](decision-log.md). The [Adaptive Media Strategy and Governance](../research/ai-artists/adaptive-media-strategy-and-governance.md) is the authoritative detailed strategy. The platform thesis provides a concise foundation, while the roadmap and durable handoff link to that canonical detail instead of reproducing it.

## Authority and Relationship to Existing Direction

This direction refines the existing source-centered adaptive-media and branded-to-direct distribution strategy. It does not replace the original Psalm and Scripture mission, historical and public-domain adaptation, permanent catalog development, traditional songs and standards, human editorial authority, rights and consent controls, or existing distribution targets.

Static catalog and dynamic event-driven media are complementary. Dynamic work does not require the removal or rejection of traditional standards, and some exceptional dynamic works may be considered for durable catalog promotion after separate review.

## Strategy Coverage

### Static Catalog and Dynamic Media

The canonical strategy preserves permanent releases, albums and artist catalogs, traditional team songs and recognized standards, seasonal catalog, dynamic event-cycle channels, temporary or disposable tracks, and review-based promotion of exceptional dynamic tracks into the durable catalog. Correction, retirement, and expiration are treated as lifecycle and provenance concerns, not as permission to erase source or decision history.

### Continuous Source-to-Lyrics Model

The strategy records a future human-governed sequence: source acquisition; source and fact verification; rights and authority review; narrative extraction; preference matching; dynamic lyric drafting; candidate generation; human review; asset and metadata preparation; internal publishing; wholesale-provider handoff; DSP ingestion and storefront availability; and feedback, correction, or refresh. Changing information is rechecked before publication. Continuous refresh does not mean automatic or unreviewed publication.

### Consumer Preference Dimensions

The documented creative dimensions are subject, team, community, genre, voice, tone, language, values and content boundaries, timing, and purpose such as recap, interpretation, preview, or education. The direction is limited to preferences people choose to express. It does not authorize audience profiling, sensitive inference, collection, or personalized delivery.

### Sports Information and Music Storytelling

The preserved creative examples are Nebraska football with country music and the Los Angeles Lakers with West Coast hip-hop and rap. They are examples only and imply no affiliation, endorsement, access, or trademark permission.

Potential lyrical subject matter includes results; winners and losers; scores; major plays; injuries; officially reported availability; coaching decisions; standings; player milestones; disputed calls; current narrative; next opponent; key matchup; and pregame anticipation. These claims require current verification, context, and human editorial review.

The recurring narrative cycle is:

```text
Post-event recap
    ->
Mid-cycle interpretation
    ->
Pre-event preview
    ->
Next event and refresh
```

### Dynamic Channel Lifecycle

The strategy includes permanent standards, seasonal catalog, event-cycle tracks, temporary recaps, preview tracks, experimental releases, review-based durable catalog promotion, and corrected, retired, or expired content. No lifecycle registry or operational status was created.

### Direct Audience Relationships and Distribution Independence

DSPs are valuable listening and discovery endpoints, not the exclusive owners of the studio's audience relationship. Company-controlled and portable audience relationships, subscription and preference concepts, audience leverage, and cross-platform continuity remain strategic objectives, not active services or data records.

Distribution independence explicitly considers catalog, audience, production, distribution, and analytics portability, supported by internal canonical identifiers. The strategy makes no claim that any asset, audience, identifier, or analytics dataset is presently portable or that a provider or platform has been selected.

### Founder Thesis and Early Implementation

The five-to-ten-year founder thesis is that distribution gates may decline, platforms may adapt, alternative platforms may gain importance, and incumbent scale does not ensure permanent relevance. This is a founder thesis, not a verified market outcome or guarantee.

The founder direction treats existing generation technology as sufficiently mature for practical early implementation, with integration and productionization as an opportunity and an intention to be among early practical implementers. First-mover advantages are plausible, not guaranteed. The Tesla analogy is bounded to integration, productionization, timing, scale, operational learning, and market execution; it does not establish equivalent engineering, safety, capital, legal, or market conditions and does not prove success.

### Provider and Company Responsibilities

Upstream providers are responsible for foundational model advancement. Generation providers are replaceable production dependencies, not the repository source of truth or owners of the company's editorial identity and audience relationship. Future records should be able to preserve provider, model/version, prompt, source inputs, voice, lineage, review, and known artifacts where applicable; no schema changed in this phase.

The company retains operational responsibility for accuracy, provenance, rights, creative direction, lyrics, identity, output selection, publication acceptance, metadata, audience alignment, distribution, corrections, catalog continuity, analytics, and portability. Publication quality includes source, lyric, identity, audio, release, and audience quality. This boundary does not assert any provider's actual capabilities, contract terms, legal obligations, or transfer of liability.

### Latency, Measurement, and Controls

Internal latency is distinct from provider and DSP latency. Potential measurements include event-to-source, source-to-verified-facts, facts-to-draft-lyrics, lyrics-to-candidate-audio, candidate-to-approval, approval-to-publication, provider latency, DSP latency, acceptance rate, correction rate, voice drift, duplicate publication, replay, skip, completion, retention, and preference-match accuracy. No metric targets were invented or established.

The detailed strategy records source verification, corrections and retractions, injury and health accuracy, defamation prevention, trademark and team/league branding, player names and publicity, sponsor references, gambling content, youth audiences, AI disclosure, false affiliation and endorsement, satire and opinion labels, expiration, archival, takedown, and human editorial authority as review topics. They are not represented as a complete legal checklist or activated policy.
